# The MVP build — the rulings, the 0.5.5 patch, and the gate before the freeze

## Context

**This one was not written in plan mode.** It is Sonic's pre-BUILD prompt of 2026-09-09 — a set
of rulings taken that morning, a patch to carry them, two read-only checks, two prepared
signatures, and an instruction to stop. It is recorded here in the same shape as the three plans
before it, because `plans/README.md` says a window with no plan file must say what directed it
instead, and this one has something better than a reconstruction: the prompt itself, ruling by
ruling, with the sections it moved.

**Where the repository stood at the start.** HEAD `f7fa0e0`, specification 0.5.4 tagged and
pushed, sixteen entities on `hedera:testnet`, 83 tests named and none expanded, 28 unfilled pins
refusing every claim. `send`, `inbox` and `verify` ran end to end against a modelled ledger and
against nothing else. Step 4 — the HCS-13 registration of §18.5's fourteen schemas — was built,
planned as 56 steps, and deliberately unsigned. Two items were open in ledger §G: who submits the
first-contact connection request, and whether §11.2's ingestion table can reach an F-3 orphan.

**The scope line for this window, ruled.** This is the **MVP BUILD**, not the full specification.
The specification goes on describing the full product; the build covers pre-funded operator paths
only. Where the two diverge, LIMITATIONS names the divergence and names **our scoping** as the
reason — never Hedera, never the specification. Deferred: the `x402-usdc` purchase leg;
Postmaster-paid carry for an agent's own submissions, so T-P4-2 is untested; the WebMCP send side;
`dns` and `nanda` in the letter path; HCS-25; rotation.

## The three roles, and the boundary that keeps them out of the code

**D-156.** OPERATOR is us, running the Postmaster: treasury, `$POSTAGE` supply key, price topic,
Postmaster-agent, counter. C1OPERATOR and C2OPERATOR run the two Correspondents and each brings a
funded testnet wallet. Beside them are the agents, Correspondent A and B, whose keys are born in
their own processes and whose accounts hold no ℏ — with one exception, below. The distinction the
words carry is §2.2's, made operational: **the agent signs, the operator pays.**

The hard half is that **the three names are demo vocabulary and never identifiers.** They may
appear in documentation, plans, ADRs and commit messages; they may not appear in `app/` as a
value, constant, default, enum member, or filename. A third party plugs in its own keys and its
own payer from its own configuration, and would otherwise find our stage directions baked into
their deployment. The repository ships a config **template** for a Correspondent and no filled
config; `app/deployment/<network>.json` stays the Postmaster's ops record and carries no
Correspondent entity.

## The ring, the payer, and the seam

**D-157**, closing ledger §G-14 — and closing it as a synthesis, for neither of the two readings
that had been stated.

§6.4 step 1 read, literally, that the Postmaster submits the connection request. §4.4's own MUST
fixes something narrower: a doorbell carries a HIP-991 fee of exactly one stamp collected by the
treasury. And the 2026-09-08 probe had observed, rather than read, that HIP-991 debits the
**transaction payer** — so "who submits" is really "whose stamp is spent."

As ruled: **the sender always signs the request; who pays for it is the sender's choice, as it is
for every other submission it makes.** The Postmaster is the default payer and what the reference
Correspondent uses. D-47 is partially superseded — the Postmaster is a payer the sender *may* use,
not one it *must*; the half that stands is that it holds no agent key, ever. §4.4's
transfer-then-ring sentence generalizes from "the Postmaster" to "the payer": one hop when the
sender pays for itself, two when it borrows, and **the ring costs the sender one stamp either
way**, which is what P-7 and §17.3 are about.

A new MUST on the Postmaster, mirroring T-P13-3 on the purchase leg: it MUST NOT submit a
connection request the requesting agent did not sign (**T-P2-4**). On the purchase leg the buyer's
signature over the body is what makes the purchase the buyer's; here the agent's signature is what
makes the ring the agent's, and the Postmaster's own signature is a payment and never an
authorship.

§6.1 also gains what it had been missing: **the Postmaster's own surface, and `carry` with it.**
The class implements the service side of `buy_stamp`, `verify` and `resolve` with the schemas of
§6, and the service side of `send` is carry — accepting transaction bodies an agent signed whose
transaction identifier names the Postmaster as payer, checking them against a published policy,
adding the payer signature, submitting, returning the consensus reference. It is not a seventh
verb: it acts on transactions rather than on envelopes, and no class is tested against it.

**The build consequence is the seam.** Every consensus submission an agent makes is constructed
*agent signs, payer signs*, with the payer an injected signer — a local key now, from the
operator's own configuration, and a remote signature through `carry` later. Nothing above the seam
knows which. In the MVP the payer is the C-operator's key, co-located in the Correspondent process;
for the ring under the seam, the agent transfers one stamp to that account and the account submits
the request the agent signed. Chunks, manifest, affix, topic creates and the account-memo update
all take the same shape.

**The doorbell watcher (D-158).** The reference Correspondent reads its own doorbell while its
process is up and answers every request that landed: the one-stamp fee is the gate P-7 names, and a
second gate on traffic the first already priced buys nothing this window. Acceptance is §7.1's —
a threshold submit key of exactly the two agents' keys, no custom fee, admin per the acceptor's
declared policy — then `connection_created` on its own doorbell. Screening policies are 0.6. *You
cannot sign for a package if you aren't home*: an agent whose process is down leaves the ringer a
slip, which is F-6 and not a refusal.

## Provisioning: one order, and two verbs that are not tools

**D-159.** Per agent: **boot** (keys generated in the Correspondent's process on first run, public
half printed, nothing on consensus) → **`buy_stamp`** with `holder` the agent's **public key**, so
the transfer auto-creates the account under that key — *the account is bought, not funded*, which
is T-P16-1's mechanism → **`generate_mailbox`** (doorbell with §4.4's fee, log, manifest topic,
HCS-2 declaration registry, HCS-1 profile file with no admin key, account memo), all owned by the
agent's keys and paid by the operator's under the seam → **fund one fee**, exactly enough ℏ for the
registration and no more, the sole exception to an agent holding no ℏ → **`register_agent`**, the
agent submitting HCS-10's `register` as its own payer and signer, which is what T-P13-4 checks and
what makes `hol` resolve it without `blurred` → **`resolve`** of its own address under `hcs14` and
under `hol`.

The order is **HCS-10's, not ours**: `register` names an account and a reader reaches the agent
through that account's memo, so registering first lists a box before the box exists.

Both new verbs live on the **Correspondent** MCP, because goose has to be able to say them. Neither
is added to §6.1's six and neither is on the conformance surface: §6.1 fixes six verbs on one noun,
and T-P15-4 requires those six to be identical across a release's transports, not that a release
offer only those six. They are documented in §4.6 as the reference implementation's provisioning
affordances.

## The orphans row

**D-160**, closing ledger §G-15 for its first reading, with a filter.

§5.10 requires `orphans` in every bundle and F-3 defines one as postage affixed where no chunk ever
lands — but §11.2's ingestion table reached a settlement by exactly one route, `hdr.st`, a field of
a chunk that by definition does not exist. §11.2 gains the row: **the treasury's inbound transfers
in the stamp token, over the window**, found from the stamp token's treasury. It is the one row not
named by something already read, and it has to be, because an unposted envelope names nothing. A
settlement is on no topic, so it is in the window by its own consensus timestamp.

**Filtered**: only settlements whose `from` is a sender in scope — a lane party, or an account
appearing as `settlement.from` among the envelopes reconciled. A stranger's orphan is not this
correspondence's business, and the sender's own retry already sees it. The filter is also what
keeps P-3 honest: an unfiltered treasury read makes a bundle a function of the whole ledger's
traffic rather than of the scope. **T-P3-6**. No schema change: `orphans` is already `[Settlement]`.

## The two read-only checks

**7a — the HOL testnet anchor `0.0.6913983`.** Sonic believed it open-submit. **Confirmed**, from
the mirror node and no broker: `submit_key: null`, `admin_key: null`, `fee_schedule_key: null`,
`custom_fees.fixed_fees: []`, `fee_exempt_key_list: []`, memo `hcs-10:0:300:3`. **No HIP-991 fee**,
so D-159's "fund one fee" covers an ordinary submission fee and nothing more. The anchor is still
at sequence 380 and its newest message is still 2025-11-18 — silent for ten months while the broker
reports operational.

Its `register` traffic does **not** match the pinned standard. At blob `0cb5d2eb`, verified with
`git hash-object`, the operation is `{p, op, account_id, m?}` with `account_id` **required** and no
`uaid` or `t_id` in the field table (`index.md:404-421`), transaction memo `hcs-10:op:0:0`. The
three newest anchor messages carry `uaid` and `t_id` and **no `account_id` at all**. WISHMail emits
the pinned shape, because T-P13-4 reads `account_id`; §9.5's rule already reads both.

**7b — the `hol` resolver's inputs.** A design note, not code, written into `app/OPERATIONS.md`:
the read path from the anchor to `properties.wishmail`, where `blurred` attaches (the registration's
payer is not the address's account — which is true of every one of the anchor's 380 messages and
will not be true of ours), and what the proof's `inputs` must carry to satisfy T-P6-1, in the shape
§5.2 fixes.

## The prepared signatures — both unsigned

**8a — the HIP-542 probe.** Prepared and reported in `app/OPERATIONS.md` with its five acts, its
named predicates, and every way it stops; **not run**. It asks two things this repository has read
about and never watched: that a token transfer to a fresh public-key alias creates the account with
the token associated and the balance credited, and that the new zero-ℏ account can then sign a
transfer *out* with the operator as payer. The second is the affix shape under the seam, and D-159's
whole order rests on it. Throwaway keys; the inert 09-08 probe token and its treasury; never the
real `$POSTAGE` and never the real doorbell.

**8b — the Step 4 freeze gate.** Ordered here deliberately: §5.11 bakes a chunk's `schemaRef` in at
assembly, so Step 4 must sign *before* the first letter, and any schema change must therefore land
*before* Step 4. **D-161** takes the last one 0.5 will ever admit — `buy_stamp` gains `provision`,
`StampReceipt` gains an optional `provisioning` line carrying the price and the entities created,
which is what §4.6 promises the agent receives: coordinates, and no secret. Admissible as a patch
only because `registeredSchemas` is null; after Step 4 the identical addition would be 0.6.

Then `npm run check:freeze`: one instance each of the three schemas the freeze would close
unexercised — StampReceipt, ReturnReceipt, ConformanceClaim with every `[fill at claim]` field
filled — and the measurement §10.4 turns on. The receipt manifest is **693 canonical bytes**, one
`ConsensusSubmitMessage`; the whole `ScheduleCreate` freezes to **862 bytes** unsigned. §10.4's
mechanism holds.

**And the check found what a check written for this purpose is supposed to find.** Nothing here had
ever validated a **manifest** against `spec/schemas/proof.schema.json`. Two findings. §5.2's
`inputs` are `{digest, locator, snapshot?}` and every manifest we wrote put the material there
directly — **fixed**, through `app/src/core/proof.ts`, and confirmed against the live declaration.
And §2.2 makes a canonical location the locator at which *the proof's own manifest* is found, which
§5.1's hashing rule and §6.4's step order make impossible — **open, ledger §G-16, and it blocks the
freeze**; `check:freeze` exits non-zero saying so, and `ack` cannot be built until it is ruled.

## The dependency order after the gate — orientation only, not authorization

Correspondent process under `app/sdk/` (boot keys, the six tools plus `generate_mailbox` and
`register_agent`, the doorbell watcher, the payer seam, the Streamable HTTP client, the one-template
logger) → the Postmaster's Streamable HTTP server → `buy_stamp`'s hbar leg with carry inside it →
the first real letter, `hcs14` to `hcs14`, with progress in the log and in the result → `send` step
7 and `ack` → the `hol` resolver, then `dns`, then `nanda` → a claim naming only what passed. The
VERIFIER-only page is downstream of a proven letter and is not touched before it.
