## What Gate Two proves, and what Friday shows

**What Gate Two proves.** That a correspondence between two agents can be reconstructed from public consensus data
alone, by someone with no key, no account, no stamp and no credential — which is the second of the two claims
WISHMail sells, and the one nothing built so far has demonstrated. Everything before it establishes only that the
parties exist: B has a mailbox and a name on a registry, and a stranger can resolve it. Gate Two adds the letter,
and with it the whole proof chain — a resolution proof welded into the envelope's AAD so that a misresolved
envelope does not open, a postmark on consensus, a return receipt the recipient signed, and a bundle a Verifier
assembles from those without asking anyone. The reply on the same lane matters as much as the first letter: it is
what shows a lane is a lane and not a transaction, that the earliest open one between two agents is *the* lane
(§7.1), and that a second letter rings no doorbell. **When `verify` runs from a third, empty home and returns the
same bundle digest the sender saw, P-4 stops being a design intention and becomes something a judge can rerun.**

**What Friday shows.** goose, by hand, driving a Correspondent's MCP — one `buy_stamp` card and one
`StampReceipt`, then `send`, `inbox`, `ack` and `verify`. Provisioning is **already on consensus** by then and is
shown as fact: the receipt card scrolled up, HashScan links open beside it, `ENTITIES.md` as the thing a judge
follows to check any of it. The minutes go to the letter loop and to the stranger's `verify`, because those are
the claims; standing up infrastructure on camera would spend the time proving that Hedera works. **Nothing is
staged**: every id shown was signed, every fee was charged, and the two things that are honestly absent — no
conformance class is claimed, and one early sale has no receipt — are said out loud rather than kept off screen.
