Found by `npm run check:prefreeze`, the pre-Step-4 sweep, which builds one resolution manifest per profile from §9's own sentences and measures the wire form — `{p, t, ...proof}`, which is what §9.1 says a manifest message carries. §9.1 requires "a manifest MUST be one HCS message at or under `CHUNK_WIRE_MAX` bytes" (T-P9-8) and §7.4 fixes `CHUNK_WIRE_MAX` at 1000 (D-96). §5.2 requires a `meaning.statement` — "the reasoner's statement of what the output means" — and bounds its length nowhere. **Nothing reconciles the two, and today the statement spends the budget.** Measured against the **live** declaration on `hedera:testnet` (account `0.0.10426206`, registry `0.0.10428113`, profile file `0.0.10428178`), with the 190-character statement the resolver actually writes: `hcs14` at an account address **997** — fits by three bytes; `hcs14` at a **real UAID read from the anchor at sequence 380**, 168 characters, **1153** — over by 153; `hcs14` under §9.2's **second form**, which carries a memo snapshot and `blurred` (D-107, and a shape T-P6-3 requires to resolve), **1056** — over by 56; `hol` at that UAID **1056** — over by 56. The sweep's own four fixtures, at the same statement: `hcs14` 1001 / 1145, `hol` 907 / 1051, `dns` 1110 / 1254, `nanda` 1231 / 1375. **So the only shape that fits is the one our own demo uses, and it fits by three bytes.** The two profiles that MUST carry a snapshot (§9.1's table; T-P6-2 rejects a non-consensus manifest without one at `send`) are over at every address. **Emptying the statement entirely brings the UAID case to 963**, so the manifest is not structurally too large — the budget is simply unallocated. **Two candidate answers, and they differ in whether Step 4 must wait.** **(a) Bound `statement`** — a `maxLength` in §5.2 and in `proof.schema.json`, with the arithmetic written down. **This is a schema change and would have to land before the freeze**, on D-161's reasoning, since `registeredSchemas` is null only until Step 4 signs. **(b) Give §9.1 its own ceiling** above `CHUNK_WIRE_MAX` — a manifest topic is not an HCS-10 topic and §7.4's constant is about HCS-10 operations (D-96), so §9.1 borrows a bound it does not have to borrow; raising it is spec text only and changes no schema. A third, **(c) drop `address` from the output**, is refused here rather than offered: §11.4 replays the output against the coordinates and §5.3 fixes `address` as "the typed address, as resolved". **The code takes neither and hides nothing**: every manifest is built as §9 says, `check:prefreeze` prints the measurements and exits 2, and no writer trims its statement to squeeze under a limit nobody has ruled on. Related to but distinct from §G-16: that one was about *where* a manifest is found, this one about *whether it fits where it is put*.
18. **OPEN — §5.3 says `resolvedAt` is "bound into the proof's inputs", and §5.2's `inputs` has nowhere to bind it (Claude Code, 2026-09-09, MINE). Blocks nothing.** Raised while landing §10.2's digest domain at 0.5.9. Sonic's wording for the exclusion was "not `resolvedAt` (in inputs)", which reads straight off §5.3's own gloss: "`resolvedAt` timestamp — query time, **as bound into the proof's inputs**". But §5.2 fixes `inputs` as `{digest, locator, snapshot?}`, closed, and none of the three is a query clock: `digest` is over what was read, `locator` is where a Verifier re-obtains it, and a `snapshot` is bytes that are not re-obtainable. §9.2's locator carries a `consensusTimestamp`, but that is **the registry entry's** timestamp — "the registry entry current at the resolution's consensus timestamp" — and not the moment the query ran. So this implementation binds `resolvedAt` nowhere in `inputs`, and §5.3's phrase is unsatisfied as written. **The exclusion is right either way** and §10.2 gives the reason that holds unconditionally: a digest containing the query's clock could never be matched by a replay at another clock, which is the whole mechanism. What is open is only §5.3's gloss. Three ways out, none ruled: **(a)** §5.3's phrase is loose and should read "query time, reported beside the coordinates" — text only; **(b)** the locator gains a `resolvedAt` field, making the phrase true and costing bytes against §9.1's budget; **(c)** `resolvedAt` is understood as already implied by the locator's `consensusTimestamp`, which is false for §9.3 and §9.4, whose locators carry a `queryTime` and a `fetchTime` respectively — and which, note, ARE the query's clock, so the two off-consensus profiles already satisfy §5.3's phrase and the two consensus ones do not. That asymmetry is probably the real finding. No schema moves for any of them and no test names it, so it blocks neither Step 4 nor the freeze.

19. **OPEN — a provisioning purchase cannot produce a `StampReceipt` that validates, because §5.4 requires the Postmaster to name two entities D-159 no longer has it create (Claude Code, 2026-09-09, MINE). BLOCKS GATE ONE's purchase; nothing else.** Found by building the counter and running the reader on the writer's output before that output could be signed (CLAUDE.md §9) — which is the whole reason that rule exists, and this time it caught the defect before a transfer rather than after one.

    **What the text says.** §5.4's shape block gives `provisioning? {price, registrationFee?, account, doorbell, log?, manifestTopic, declRegistry?, profileFile?}`, and `spec/schemas/stamp-receipt.schema.json` makes `price`, `account`, `doorbell` and `manifestTopic` **required**. §5.4's sentence says why: "the fields after it are the entities **the Postmaster created for the holder**". And §6.3's postcondition is "a `StampReceipt` whose `txRef` is the transfer, carrying a `provisioning` line **exactly when** `provision` was true".

    **What the build says.** D-159 as amended (2026-09-09) makes the purchase one atomic transaction with three legs — ℏ from the buyer, `$POSTAGE` from the treasury to the agent's public-key alias, and the registration fee in ℏ to that same alias — and moves `generate_mailbox` onto the **Correspondent's** MCP, where the agent creates its own six topics under its own key, **after** the purchase. So the Postmaster creates exactly one entity for the holder: the account. It cannot name a doorbell or a manifest topic, because at receipt time neither exists and neither will be its.

    **Both branches contradict a sentence.** A receipt carrying the line fails its own registered schema and a Verifier rejects it. A receipt omitting the line contradicts §6.3's "exactly when", and takes `registrationFee` with it — which is the field D-159's addendum added precisely so "a Verifier sees it as a leg of the purchase and not as a gift". There is no third branch: §14.3 forbids charging under an unpublished price, so the 2 ℏ cannot be taken with `provision` false either.

    **What the code does, and does not do.** It does not code around it. `counter/purchase.ts::provisioningFieldsThisCounterCannotFill` reads the required list **out of the registered schema** and refuses a `provision: true` purchase **before anything is signed**, naming this row; a `provision: false` purchase works in full. Because the gate reads the schema rather than restating it, a 0.6 that changes the schema lifts the refusal by itself, with no second place to remember. `npm run check:correspondent` asserts the two missing fields by name.

    **Two candidate answers, and they differ in whether a minor version is needed.**
    **(a) The Postmaster provisions the topics after all** — §4.6's provisioned path taken literally, with the Postmaster paying and the agent signing each topic creation over the counter, as Steps 2 and 3 already do for the Postmaster's own agent. **No schema moves.** The cost is that `buy_stamp` becomes a multi-round-trip agent-signed exchange, and CLAUDE.md §11's placement of `generate_mailbox` on the Correspondent's MCP is amended.
    **(b) `doorbell` and `manifestTopic` become optional inside `provisioning`**, present exactly where the Postmaster created them, with §5.4's sentence widened to say that D-159 splits the provisioned path into a leg the Postmaster sells — the account and the fee — and a leg the agent performs itself. **This is a change to a registered schema, so it is 0.6 and not a patch (§1.7), and the fourteen are frozen on consensus as of 2026-09-09.** It is the smaller change to the text and the larger one to the version.
    A third, *drop the receipt's provisioning line and price the account some other way*, is refused here rather than offered: §14.3 has one schedule for everyone and the provisioned path is on it.
