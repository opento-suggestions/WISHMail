## Context

Either party to a lane can submit under its threshold key, and an envelope's identifier is public from the settlement's memo before any chunk lands — so a third slice bearing the right `id` and index could be injected.

## Decision

The record, from ledger §B row D-87, verbatim:

> **The chain.** Every chunk but the last carries `nx` = SHA-256 of the next slice; `h` moves into chunk 0's header. Canonical chunk 0 = earliest whose header rebuilds to `id` (unforgeable before it is public; a copy cannot precede what it copies); canonical chunk *i* = earliest whose slice hashes to canonical *i−1*'s `nx`. Off-chain chunks are recorded and unused whenever they land. Closes the lane-peer griefing found while drafting §11 (a recipient could front-run a slice, since `id` is public from the settlement memo and `h` rode on every chunk) at zero wire cost on later chunks. Chunk-0-last submission ordering, proposed by the prior session's read, does *not* close it and was not adopted. Amends §2.2, §5.6, §6.5, §7.4, §8.5 (`CHANGED`); T-P3-2, T-P3-3 revised; T-P1-11 added. Sonic: the same mechanism is used in his `ontologic-hello-world` (opento-suggestions; not fetchable by Claude, robots-disallowed — alignment of field name/direction unverified).

## Alternatives considered

- None recorded. The ledger §B row states the decision without a fork; where a fork was closed, the row says so.

## Consequences

**The chain.** Every chunk but the last carries `nx`, the digest of the next slice; `h` moves into chunk 0's header. Canonical chunk 0 is the earliest whose header rebuilds to `id` — unforgeable before it is public, and a copy cannot precede what it copies, since consensus ordering is total. Each later chunk is the earliest whose slice hashes to the prior's `nx`. Reassembly becomes a walk on bytes rather than on clocks (§7.4, §11.3, T-P1-11, T-P3-3).
