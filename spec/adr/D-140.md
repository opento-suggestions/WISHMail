## Context

The specification names the Postmaster as a role and the treasury as an account, and never says how many accounts a deployment uses. §4.1 puts the unissued supply in "the treasury account"; §4.3 has affixed postage transferred "to the treasury"; §4.4 has the doorbell fee "collected by the treasury"; §14.1 puts the return-receipt fee inside postage, so also to the treasury. §3.5 makes the Postmaster the payer of carriage and the holder of the supply key. §14.2 makes the Postmaster the receiver of the money leg of a purchase. One account could do all of it. The question is whether it should.

## Decision

**Three roles, three accounts, three distinct keys** (RECORD, Sonic 2026-09-07: "Three roles, three accounts, distinct keys").

| Role | Account | Key | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operator / payer** | `0.0.8641261`, reused | pre-existing, `.env` as `OPERATOR_*` | Pays the network fee for everything the Postmaster carries and provisions (§3.5, §4.6, D-47). Is `payTo` for the money leg of a purchase (§14.2). Is the momentary bearer of the first-contact stamp (§4.4). Holds the price topic's submit and admin keys (D-142). |
| **Treasury** | created here | generated in-process, `.env` as `TREASURY_*` | Holds the unissued `$POSTAGE` supply (§4.1). Receives affixed postage (§4.3), doorbell fees (§4.4), and the return-receipt fee inside postage (§14.1). Signs the stamp leg of every `buy_stamp` (§14.2). |
| **Postmaster-agent** | created here | generated in-process, `.env` as `AGENT_*` | The service's own Correspondent identity: its own account, doorbell, log, and manifest topic, provisioned by the same path a customer's agent takes (§4.6). |

**The operator account is reused, and its residue is stated rather than cleaned** (RECORD, Sonic: "Reuse 0.0.8641261"). It carries 17 colour tokens and 2 USDC from prior work. "Start Fresh" (CLAUDE.md §2) binds *code* — "Do not import, port, or paraphrase code" — not accounts; an account is a testnet coordinate, not a line of authorship. FETCHED 2026-09-07 from `https://testnet.mirrornode.hedera.com/api/v1/accounts/0.0.8641261`: balance 1,610.729 HBAR, key type ED25519, `max_automatic_token_associations: 20` against 18 tokens already associated. The residue is inert — no fixture resolves to it and no price list names any of those tokens — and §4.1's "No token other than the stamp is postage" is a rule about what a Postmaster accepts as postage, not about what an account may happen to hold.

**The treasury's key is hot, and the split is containment and accounting, not cold custody** (RECORD, Sonic: "Note in the ADR that its key is hot (it signs the stamp leg of every buy_stamp); the split is containment and accounting, not cold custody"). §14.2's purchase is atomic: the buyer's money moves to the operator and the stamps move out of the treasury in one transaction, so the treasury key must be available on every purchase. Nothing here is offline. What the split buys is that the account holding every stamp in existence is not the account paying every network fee — a compromise of one is not automatically a compromise of the other — and that the books read cleanly: postage in and postage out are one account's history, carriage costs are another's.

**The Postmaster-agent identity is an operational choice, not a spec role.** Stated in so many words, because a reviewer should not have to reconstruct it:

§4.6 says "an agent needs an account, a doorbell, a log, a manifest topic, and a declaration under at least one profile" — written of *an agent*. §1.4 gives the POSTMASTER class only the service side of `buy_stamp`, `send` and `verify`: **no `inbox`, no `ack`, no doorbell, no profile obligation.** A Postmaster that never corresponds needs none of this. This deployment gives itself a Correspondent identity anyway, because the reference implementation dogfoods the path it sells and because the demo has the service's own agent corresponding — and §1.4 expressly permits it: "An implementation MAY claim more than one class."

**P-13 is not violated, and the reason is not that we are careful.** P-13 and §3.5 forbid the Postmaster to hold *an agent's* private key — an agent being a counterparty whose keys are born in its own process and stay there (§4.6). The Postmaster-agent's keys are **this organization's own**, held by the party that generated them, for an identity it operates. No customer's key is held, and none ever will be. The distinction is not merely verbal, because T-P13-1 tests *the service's key store and code path* rather than an intention. So the form the build must honour is structural:

- the agent's key lives in `.env` under an `AGENT_*` namespace **that no Postmaster service code path reads**;
- the provisioning script **does not special-case its own agent** (RECORD, Sonic: "the provisioning script must not special-case its own agent") — it provisions the Postmaster-agent through exactly the code a customer's agent goes through, so any shortcut taken for ourselves would be a shortcut shipped to everyone;
- no doorbell, log, or manifest topic anywhere carries an operator key as admin or submit key (D-47, D-138's table).

If those three hold, the claim is checkable by grep and by mirror-node readback, which is how it will be checked.

## Alternatives considered

- **One account for everything.** MINE, rejected: it makes the supply-holding account the fee-paying account, so the accounting cannot be read apart and one key compromise takes both. The specification permits it; nothing recommends it.
- **A cold treasury with a hot spending account.** Rejected as a fiction here: §14.2's atomic purchase needs the treasury key on every buy, so a "cold" treasury would sign constantly. Better to say the key is hot than to imply custody that does not exist.
- **No Postmaster-agent identity at all.** MINE, rejected: the reference implementation would then never exercise the provisioning path it sells, and the demo needs an agent the service can speak as. Recorded because it is the *conformant* option — §1.4 requires nothing of the sort — so that exceeding it reads as a choice and not an oversight.
- **Give the Postmaster-agent its identity by reusing the operator account.** Rejected: it would put the operator's key on a doorbell and a log, which D-47 and D-138's table forbid outright.

## Consequences

Two accounts are created in this phase, and before the token, because §4.1 sets a token's treasury at creation and the treasury account must therefore already exist. Each is created with a key generated in the provisioning process and written only to `.env`; `.env.example` gains the names with blank values. Where each key was generated and where it lives is recorded per role in `app/OPERATIONS.md`, and no key material enters `spec/pins.json`, the ops record, a commit, or a log.

The operator is associated with `$POSTAGE` **explicitly**, rather than leaning on its two remaining automatic association slots, because §4.4 has the sender transfer one stamp *to the Postmaster*, which then pays the doorbell fee — a path that must not depend on a slot count other work could consume. The treasury needs no association: a token's treasury is associated by construction, which is also what makes it a safe fee collector, and that is the property the Step 2.5 probe confirms rather than assumes.

The **tail risk on the treasury key is stated in D-141**, not here, because it follows from the token's key posture rather than from the account structure.
