# D-172 — T-P1-8 asks the ledger for what the ledger can show, and it is stronger than what it asked before

Status:      accepted
Date:        2026-09-10
Decided by:  Sonic
Drafted by:  Claude Code

Tests:       none added. **T-P1-8's sketch is amended**; the register stays at 86.
Supersedes:  none
Spec marker: CHANGED: D-172 on §10.4, §11.4

## Context

T-P1-8 asked for a fixture receipt whose "schedule record shows **exactly** the
recipient's signature". It cannot exist.

A ScheduleCreate's signatures are offered to the *inner* transaction's required
keys, and §10.4's inner transaction requires two: the recipient's, because the
manifest goes to a topic only the recipient's key can write to, and the inner
**payer's**, because someone has to pay for the execution and §10.4 forbids that
being the recipient (T-P16-2). So the record can never show one signature unless
the recipient is the payer, which §10.4 itself refuses.

**And the ledger records more than that.** FETCHED 2026-09-10: twenty-five
executed schedules read from `/api/v1/schedules?limit=25&order=desc` each carried
**two** signatures. Our own schedule `0.0.10465145`, read from the mirror after it
executed, carries **three**:

```
0.0.10450879  at the ScheduleCreate   A2's operator — the inner payer
0.0.10452127  at the ScheduleSign     B's agent — the required key
0.0.10450880  at the same instant     B's own operator — paid for the ScheduleSign,
                                      and required nothing
```

HIP-423 records **every transaction payer that touches a schedule**, not only the
keys the inner transaction required. The count is a function of who paid, not of
who was needed. Raised as ledger §G-22 before the gate, amended by observation
after the run, and coded around at no point: §11.4's own wording was already a
membership test, and the membership test is what the implementation checks.

## Decision

**RECORD (Sonic, 2026-09-10).** HIP-423 wins. T-P1-8 is amended to what the
ledger can show, which is stronger than what it said:

- the schedule **executed**;
- the recipient's key is **among** the signatures;
- the scheduled transaction's **required** signer is exactly the submit key of
  the recipient's manifest topic, so it **could not have executed under anyone
  else's signature**;
- the execution timestamp follows the nth chunk's, and the executed submission's
  postmark is on the recipient's manifest topic.

Payer signatures are bookkeeping. §11.4's "among the signatures" stays the check.
§10.4's MUST — "signed by the recipient's key alone" — keeps its words and gains
the reading that makes them true: *alone* is said of what **completes** the
schedule, which is what the network enforces; a ledger may additionally record
who paid for each transaction touching it, and those entries neither complete it
nor disqualify it.

**What replaced the discarded property is better than it.** "Exactly one
signature" was a statement about the shape of a record. "The required signer is
the manifest topic's submit key" is a statement about what the network would
**refuse to execute** — the mechanism enforces it rather than merely recording
it. A record can be miscounted; a required key cannot be talked past.

## Why this is a patch, 0.5.11, and not 0.6

**First and structurally.** The test as written was **unsatisfiable**: there is no
arrangement of payers and signers, conformant to §10.4, that produces a record
showing exactly the recipient's signature. **Nothing could ever have passed it**,
so correcting it takes nothing away from anyone, and §1.7's bar — invalidating a
previously conforming implementation — cannot be reached by amending a test no
implementation could satisfy.

**Second, as fact.** No test body is expanded and no release claims any class.

**Third, as precedent.** D-157 and D-169: both patches after the freeze, both
touching neither a wire string nor a registered schema. Neither does this.

## Alternatives considered

- **Keep "exactly" and make the sender's operator the inner payer of a schedule
  it does not sign.** MINE, rejected before it was proposed: it does not work.
  The ScheduleCreate's own payer signature is recorded whatever the arrangement,
  and so is any ScheduleSign payer's.
- **Read "exactly" as "exactly the recipient's, among those that were
  required".** MINE, rejected: it rescues the words by reading them as something
  they do not say, and the run showed a third signature that was required by
  nothing at all, so even the rescued reading is false of the record.
- **Leave T-P1-8 as it stands and note the divergence.** Rejected (RECORD): a
  test nothing can pass is not a court, and §1.3 has no requirement without one.

## Consequences

- §10.4's MUST gains the reading of *alone*, and its `Conformance:` note is
  rewritten; §11.4's receipt paragraph says "**a** signature" and states the
  required-signer property.
- `verify` is unchanged: it already matched the recipient's key against the
  record's prefixes and already checked that the inner topic's submit key is the
  recipient's. This decision makes the specification say what the code does and
  what the network enforces.
- The `receipt` half of the appraisal is unaffected: `acked`, `unclaimed`,
  `invalid`, `none` keep their meanings, and §11.5's "the receipt does not lower
  it" is untouched.
