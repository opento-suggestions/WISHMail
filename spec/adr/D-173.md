# D-173 — The evidence bundle's `spec` is the minor version, and the Verifier's own patch is an observation

Status:      accepted
Date:        2026-09-10
Decided by:  Sonic
Drafted by:  Claude Code
Sections:    §5.10, §11.6, §11.7
Tests:       none added. **T-P3-1's sketch is amended**; the register stays at 86.
Supersedes:  none
Spec marker: CHANGED: D-173 on §5.10, §11.6, §11.7

## Context

§11.7 requires that "two Verifiers reconciling the same scope and window MUST
produce evidence with the same digest", and T-P3-1 is the court: replay by a
fresh Verifier "equals the Postmaster's evidence for the fixture correspondence,
byte for byte".

§5.10's `EvidenceBundle` carries `spec`, the digest is SHA-256 over the evidence
including it, and `verify.ts` filled it from `RELEASE.spec` — the full
`major.minor.patch`. So the digest was a function of the patch revision of
whichever Verifier computed it.

**This was found by a version bump and not by reading.** Raising the release from
0.5.10 to 0.5.11 failed `check:captured` and `check:receipt` on their digests
with **no change whatever to the evidence**. The cause was isolated rather than
assumed: with the whole of D-171's code in place and `RELEASE.spec` alone set
back to `'0.5.10'`, both digests returned to exactly the values the network had
produced — `8d30dfdc…` and `00229e6f…` — and both checks passed. Raised as ledger
§G-25 and not coded around; `RELEASE.spec` was left as it stood and the fixtures'
recorded digests were left untouched.

**Two independent implementations are never at one patch revision.** So as the
text stood, the one property P-3 exists to give — that a stranger's Verifier and
the Postmaster's produce the same digest over the same consensus data — held
between two implementations only by accident.

## Decision

**RECORD (Sonic, 2026-09-10).** The evidence bundle's `spec` carries the **minor
version** — `0.5` — like every other cross-implementation string §1.7 governs.
The patch a Verifier ran at is a fact about the Verifier and not about the
evidence, and it moves to `observations.verifierSpec`, which §11.6 already
excludes from the digest for exactly this reason.

§1.7 supplies the reason and not merely the precedent: "the wire carries
`major.minor` and nothing finer", and "the schemas registered under HCS-13 for
that minor version are its schemas". A bundle's evidence is computed against
those schemas and that text, so the minor version is what identifies its shape.
A patch revision identifies only who was reading.

**T-P3-1's sketch says so explicitly**: replay "at a different patch revision of
the same minor version" equals the reference bundle byte for byte. The test now
demands the property it was written to demand.

## Why this is a patch, 0.5.12, and not 0.6 — and the version was PROBED, not argued

**RECORD (Sonic, 2026-09-10):** whether the ruling lands as 0.5.12 or as 0.6 "is
decided by probing the frozen `evidence-bundle` schema rather than by argument",
because a change to a registered schema is 0.6 by §1.7's freeze and is never
coded around.

**The probe, and the file was verified before it was trusted.** sha256 over the
raw git blob bytes of `spec/schemas/evidence-bundle.schema.json` is
`248fdaae31aeb761aa5474f497d2219804b0e41569eb0120356d91e3a7e0f912`, equal to the
digest `spec/pins.json` records for the schema registered on HCS-13 at
`hcs://13/0.0.10448537#1`; and the working tree is byte-identical to that blob.
So what was read is the registered schema and not a working copy that had
drifted from it.

**What it constrains** — `spec/schemas/evidence-bundle.schema.json:9-11`:

```json
    "spec": {
      "type": "string"
    },
```

A bare `"type": "string"`. **No `pattern`, no `format`, no `enum`, no `const`,
and no description.** `"0.5"` is admissible to it exactly as `"0.5.11"` is, and
**no schema moves.** (The same is true of the `spec` field in
`conformance-claim.schema.json` and `price-list.schema.json`; neither is touched
here, and the published price lists are records and are not rewritten.)

So the version question falls to §1.7's ordinary bar, and this clears it three
ways.

**First and structurally.** §1.7's bar is "a change to a conformance test that
would invalidate a previously conforming implementation". T-P3-1 compares *two
implementations*, and two independent implementations are never at one patch
revision — so as written it could be satisfied only by accident, and it fixed no
determinate conforming behaviour that this takes away. It is the same shape of
defect D-172 closed for T-P1-8: a test nothing could pass, made passable.

**Second, as fact.** `RELEASE.classes` is empty, no test body is expanded, and no
release claims any class — so there is no previously conforming implementation at
all.

**Third, as precedent.** D-157 added a MUST inside a patch, D-169 fixed a
comparison rule inside one, and D-171 and D-172 both landed inside 0.5.11 — every
one after the freeze, and every one touching neither a wire string nor a
registered schema. Neither does this.

**§1.7's own list of wire strings is untouched, deliberately.** `spec/pins.json`'s
`wireStrings` gains nothing. The bundle's `spec` is not a string on the wire
between two agents; it is the identifier of the text a piece of evidence was
computed under, which is why §5.10 and §11.7 are where this is said and §1.7 is
only cited.

## `observations.verifierSpec` — the two things proved before the field was written

**RECORD (Sonic, 2026-09-10):** prove both, or drop `verifierSpec` entirely and
let the narrative carry the Verifier's patch revision instead. Both hold.

**(1) `observations` lies outside the digested bundle.** The specification says
it three times. §5.10's diagram gives the field as "sha256 over the bundle with
`digest` and `observations` absent". §5.10's prose: "Everything above `observations` is
evidence … `observations` is what a Verifier saw at its own clock … and is
excluded from the digest because two Verifiers at two times cannot agree on it".
§11.7: "The digest is SHA-256 over the evidence with `digest` and `observations`
absent, and is the bundle's identifier." §11.6: an observation is "reported under
`observations` (§5.10), excluded from the evidence digest, and without bearing on
any state or standing."

The code does not merely honour that — it makes the mistake unavailable.
[verify.ts:986-995](app/src/tools/verify.ts#L986-L995) builds `evidence` from six
keys, none of them `observations`, and digests exactly that object; the
`observations` object is not constructed until
[verify.ts:997](app/src/tools/verify.ts#L997), **after** the digest exists, and
the bundle is assembled from all three at
[verify.ts:1007](app/src/tools/verify.ts#L1007). `observations` cannot enter the
digest because at the moment the digest is taken there is no such value.
Independently: the three fixture checks recompute the recorded network digests by
destructuring `observations` and `digest` back out
([captured.check.ts:139](app/src/tools/captured.check.ts#L139)), and those
recomputations equal the digests consensus produced — so the evidence the network
was digested over demonstrably excluded observations.

**(2) The frozen schema admits the key.** Verified against the pinned blob and
not the working tree: `git cat-file blob HEAD:spec/schemas/evidence-bundle.schema.json`
digests to `248fdaae31aeb761aa5474f497d2219804b0e41569eb0120356d91e3a7e0f912`,
equal to `spec/pins.json`. In that blob, `properties.observations` is a plain
`"type": "object"` with `required` `["appraisedAt","mirror","drift","disagreement"]`
and declared properties `appraisedAt, mirror, drift, disagreement, agentIdOrder,
integrity` — and **no `additionalProperties`, no `unevaluatedProperties`, no
`propertyNames`, no `patternProperties`, and no combinator**. Under JSON Schema
2020-12 an undeclared key is therefore permitted, and **no schema moves.**

**Read, then run** — §9's rule, applied to a schema rather than to a resolver.
The frozen schema's own compiled validator was run over three objects before any
code was changed:

```
VALIDATES  a bundle with spec "0.5" and no verifierSpec
VALIDATES  the same bundle with observations.verifierSpec "0.5.12"
REFUSED    the same key placed BESIDE observations, at the top level
             / must NOT have additional properties
```

The third line is not decoration. The bundle's **top level** carries
`additionalProperties: false`, so the Verifier's patch revision could not have
been reported as a sibling of `spec` without a schema change — which would be
0.6. `observations` is not where this field was put for tidiness; it is the only
place inside the frozen schema where it can go, and it is also the only place
§11.6 would allow it. `check:letter` validates every bundle it produces against
this schema ([letter.check.ts:426](app/src/tools/letter.check.ts#L426),
[letter.check.ts:1264](app/src/tools/letter.check.ts#L1264)), so the court that
would catch a regression here already exists and runs on every battery.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave `RELEASE.spec` in the bundle and narrow §11.7's MUST to one patch
  revision.** **Rejected (RECORD).** It would make P-3 a promise between copies
  of one implementation rather than between two, which is the opposite of what
  §3.7 and P-3 are for.
- **Silently change `RELEASE.spec` to the minor version.** MINE, rejected before
  it was proposed: it resolves a specification question by editing an
  implementation constant, and the constant is honest — this release *is* at a
  patch revision.
- **Drop `spec` from the evidence and report it only in `observations`.**
  MINE, not taken: `spec` is in the frozen schema's `required` list, so removing
  it is 0.6, and a reader of a bundle should be able to see which specification
  it was computed under without consulting an undigested field.

## Consequences

- §5.10's `EvidenceBundle` prose says what `spec` carries and why; its
  `observations` line gains `verifierSpec?`. §11.6 records the Verifier's patch
  revision beside its clock and its mirror. §11.7's MUST says the agreement
  reaches across patch revisions, and T-P3-1's `Conformance:` note says it too.
- `verify.ts` fills `spec` from `RELEASE.minorVersion`, which already existed for
  §1.7's sake, and reports `RELEASE.spec` under `observations.verifierSpec`.
- **Every digest already written into a run of record moves once, permanently,
  and then never moves again for a patch.** The recorded values stay true of the
  release that computed them and reproduce from that release's tag —
  `8d30dfdc…` and `00229e6f…` from `v0.5.10`, `1c4359e5…` from `v0.5.11`. Each
  run of record now carries a dated note saying so **beside its own number**, and
  so does LIMITATIONS L-1, so that a judge who clones HEAD and re-runs `verify`
  on those lanes meets the reason before they meet a different digest. **The
  runs of record are not rewritten** — a record gains a dated note; it is not
  edited to agree with a later day.
- `check:captured`, `check:receipt` and `check:reply` keep the spec-string
  substitution they gained on 2026-09-10: those fixtures were captured under
  `"0.5.10"` and `"0.5.11"` and are **records**. Beside them a new assertion
  proves the property this decision creates — a bundle made now carries `"0.5"`,
  and flipping `RELEASE.spec` to another patch of 0.5 leaves the digest exactly
  where it was.
- Closes ledger **§G-25**, its finding kept unedited beneath the ruling.
