## Context

§1.6 carried both HIPs as `Final · n/a`, and `spec/pins.json` mirrored that as `"pin": "n/a"`. A HIP is not a Draft, so the omission looked harmless. It was not.

Everything the doorbell of §4.4 depends on rests on HIP-991, and every one of those properties entered this repository as **second-hand assertion in our own ADRs** rather than as a quoted sentence:

- `spec/adr/D-049.md:21` — "Q-12 closed: the stamp is fungible (HIP-991 fees are HBAR or fungible HTS only; simplest)."
- `spec/adr/D-049.md:25` — a non-fungible stamp "rejected on the measurement: a HIP-991 custom fee is payable in HBAR or a fungible HTS token only, so a non-fungible stamp could not pay a doorbell."
- `spec/adr/D-020.md:27` — "The measurement arrived with HIP-991: a custom fee is payable in HBAR or a fungible HTS token only."
- `spec/adr/D-044.md:15` — "HIP-991 collects a custom fee in one token, and P-11 requires one price for every buyer."

Q-12 was closed on that measurement, and the stamp is fungible because of it (§4.1, L-12). CLAUDE.md §3 requires a fetch before asserting anything about a pinned standard; the doorbell was about to be built against an inference, which is the F-9 failure mode.

Sonic, 2026-09-07: "fetch it and fill the pin … Every property the doorbell depends on (fee in a fungible HTS token, exempt-key list, fee schedule key) is secondhand from our own ADRs. One fetch, line cites, pin filled. Non-negotiable in the F-9 sense." RECORD.

## Decision

`spec/pins.json`'s `hips` block is filled to the same shape as the standards pins. The digest recipe is the one the file already states: SHA-256 over the raw git blob bytes, never over a working-tree checkout.

The sentences the doorbell is built on, now quotable, are recorded in ledger §H with their line numbers. The three ADRs above **gain the citation beside their inference and are not edited away** — a superseded or under-evidenced reasoning is kept, because the reasoning that was later shored up is the part a reader needs.

HIP-423 is pinned in the same change though nothing currently turns on its text, because §1.6 carried both on the same `n/a` footing and pinning one and not the other would leave the table inconsistent for no reason.

**Version: 0.5.1.** A patch. §1.7: a patch "amends text and tests within a minor version; it changes no wire string, and once a minor version's schemas are registered it changes no schema." No wire string moves.

## Alternatives considered

- **Record the fetch in ledger §H and leave §1.6 saying `n/a`.** Claude Code's lean, MINE, on the ground that §H carries it immediately and nothing is blocked either way. Rejected by Sonic: the pin is the point. §H is where a fetch is dated; §1.6 is where conformance is measured, and a standard the doorbell's MUST depends on belongs there.
- **Pin HIP-991 only.** Rejected: §1.6 carried both as `n/a`, and a table with one HIP pinned and one not invites the question at every reading.
- **Pin at `main` rather than at a commit.** Rejected on §1.6's own definition — "the git blob of the standard's file at a named commit." `main` is not a pin. (The two are byte-identical today, which is why the last-touching commit is the honest anchor.)

## Consequences

§1.6 gains four lines and the repository gains its first `CHANGED` marker, which is what D-131 set the convention up for. `spec/pins.json` gains two filled entries; they are **not** new nulls, so T-P9-2's count is unchanged and the thirty remaining nulls are still what block every claim.

Every later statement about HIP-991 in this repository cites `hip-991.md` at this blob rather than an ADR. D-138's "no fee schedule key" rests on `:97`, and D-137's exemption reasoning rests on `:110-113`; neither would have been safe to write from the prior inference, and `:97` in particular — that a topic created without a fee schedule key can never gain one — was not recorded anywhere before this fetch.

One property remains unverified and is not closed here: **HIP-991 says nothing about whether a fee collector must be associated with the denominating token.** Neither does the SDK documentation. Ledger §H records it as MINE, and the provisioning probe settles it empirically before any doorbell is created.
