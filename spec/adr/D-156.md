## Context

The demo has three people in it and the repository had no word for any of them.
§2.2 defines **Operator** — "the human or organization behind an agent" — and
§3.3 says the Operator is visible to the correspondence, but nothing named the
difference between the operator who runs the *Postmaster* and the operators who
run the two Correspondents, and nothing said where their keys and wallets come
from.

That gap is where a demo label becomes a defect. A value like `C1OPERATOR`
written into `app/` as a constant, a default, an enum member or a filename would
ship in the release, and the third party who plugs in their own keys would find
our stage directions baked into their deployment. The same hazard, one level
down, is the reason `.env*` is gitignored and `npm run p13:check` exists.

## Decision

**Three human roles, named for the demo and for the repository's prose.**

- **OPERATOR** — us. Runs the Postmaster: the treasury, the `$POSTAGE` supply
  key, the price topic, the Postmaster-agent, the counter.
- **C1OPERATOR**, **C2OPERATOR** — the two Correspondents' operators. Each
  brings a funded testnet wallet.
- **Correspondent A / B** — the agents. Each holds its own keys, born in its own
  process (P-13, §3.3). An agent's account never holds ℏ, with the single
  exception of D-159's one registration fee.

The distinction the words carry is the one §2.2 already draws, made
operational: **the agent signs, the operator pays.**

**The three names are demo vocabulary and MUST NOT be identifiers.** They belong
in documentation, plans, ADRs, STATUS, LIMITATIONS, and commit messages. They
must not appear in `app/` as a value, constant, default, enum member, or
filename. Every private key, every payer wallet, and every operator-specific
value the Correspondent MCP reads comes from that operator's own configuration
file or environment. The repository ships a config **template** for a
Correspondent; a filled config is gitignored. `app/deployment/<network>.json`
stays the Postmaster's ops record and carries no Correspondent entity ID.

## Alternatives considered

- **Leave the roles unnamed and say "the two agents".** Lost because the ring,
  the payer seam (D-157) and the provisioning order (D-159) each turn on which
  party signs and which pays; prose that cannot name them cannot state them.
- **Ship a filled example config with throwaway keys.** Rejected: a filled
  config in the tree is a key in the tree the moment someone edits it in place,
  and P-13's gate is a grep, not a judgment.
- **Name the roles in the specification.** Rejected: they are a deployment's
  cast. §2.2's Operator is the normative word, and the specification does not
  learn our demo.

## Consequences

`CLAUDE.md` gains §11, whose first two paragraphs are this decision. No
specification sentence changes; no schema, no test, no version change on its own
account.

What it binds going forward: the Correspondent process under `app/sdk/` reads
its account, its payer and its network from its own configuration and from
nothing else, and the config template it ships is the only config in the tree.
