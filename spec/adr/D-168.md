# D-168 — The Postmaster provisions the mailbox it sells, and the payer seam's remote half is exercised

Status:      accepted
Date:        2026-09-09
Decided by:  Sonic
Drafted by:  Claude Code
Sections:    none. §4.6, §5.4, §6.1 and §6.3 are read as written; no sentence changes.
Tests:       none added. T-P13-1, T-P13-3, T-P13-4, T-P16-1 and T-P17-1 are what the arrangement is built against.
Supersedes:  D-159's third rejected alternative, and the clause of its step 3 that names the operator as payer
Spec marker: none — no specification text changes

## Context

Ledger §G item 19, raised the same day: **a provisioning purchase could not
produce a `StampReceipt` that validates.**

§5.4 makes `doorbell` and `manifestTopic` required inside `provisioning`, and it
says why in its own words — "the fields after it are the entities **the
Postmaster created for the holder**". §6.3 makes the line present "exactly when
`provision` was true". But D-159 as amended had the Postmaster create exactly one
entity for the holder, the account, and had the agent create its own six topics
afterwards under its own key, through `generate_mailbox` on the Correspondent's
own MCP.

Every branch contradicted a sentence. A receipt carrying the line failed the
schema Step 4 registered under HCS-13 and a Verifier would reject it. A receipt
omitting the line contradicted §6.3's "exactly when", and took `registrationFee`
with it — the field D-159's own addendum added so that a Verifier sees the fee
"as a leg of the purchase and not as a gift". And §14.3 closed the third door:
the 2 ℏ cannot be charged with `provision` false, because that price is published
under `provisioning`.

The finding was raised rather than coded around (CLAUDE.md §5), and it was found
because the reader was run on the writer's output **before that output could be
signed** (CLAUDE.md §9) — which is what that rule is for, and this time it caught
the defect before a transfer rather than after one.

## Decision

**Ruled (a): the Postmaster provisions the mailbox it sells** — §4.6's
provisioned path taken as written. §5.4's sentence is then literally true of the
receipt, and the receipt validates against the frozen schema.

**It is not a workaround and this ADR does not call it one.** It needs nothing
from the specification and it is the path §4.6 named first. What recommends the
alternative is accuracy about history, not about the text.

**How it is built, and what did NOT move.**

- **The provisioner did not move into the counter.** `sdk/mailbox.ts` creates the
  six rows in the same forced order, signed by the same agent key, with the same
  readbacks, and it does not know it is being carried. `Session.payer` is an
  injected `Signer`, and under a provisioning purchase that signer is **remote**:
  for each frozen body the agent has signed, the Correspondent sends the body
  bytes and the purchase reference to the counter, and receives the Postmaster's
  payer signature. The transaction id names the Postmaster as payer.
- **This is §6.1's carry, exactly** (D-157): agent-signed bodies, the
  Postmaster's account as payer, a published policy, a co-signature. CLAUDE.md
  §11 asked for the payer to be built "as a seam and not as a shortcut"; the
  remote half is now exercised rather than merely allowed for.
- **The counter's policy is structural**, reads `ops/template.ts`, and decodes
  the very bytes it signs. Under an outstanding provisioning reference whose
  holder key is K, it signs a body only if it is (i) a topic create that is
  exactly row *k* of the template with K as the keys that row names, (ii) an
  HCS-1 chunk on the file topic it itself paid to create under this reference,
  (iii) the HCS-2 register entry on the registry it paid for, or (iv) the
  account-memo update on the holder's account, naming that registry and setting
  nothing else. Anything else is refused, no mark is left (§3.5), and the reason
  is a `STAMP_*` code as §6.3 fixes them.
- **The counter never sees a private key and never builds a provisioning body**
  (P-13, T-P13-1). It receives bytes the agent has already signed and decides
  whether to pay for them.
- **The receipt is issued from the counter's own readback**, only once every row
  has landed *and* the holder resolves under §9.2 from the counter's reader, and
  it is validated against the registered schema before it is returned. Until
  then the reference is outstanding.
- **`buy_stamp` with `provision` does the whole thing** and returns the receipt:
  quote → the three-legged transfer → `generateMailbox` under the remote payer →
  the receipt. `generate_mailbox` remains on the Correspondent as the
  **self-provisioned** affordance, for an agent that brings its own account and
  pays for its own mailbox at its operator's expense; the demo does not say it,
  and its tool description says which path it is.

**Idempotency, refined from D-165.** `buy_stamp` with `provision` refuses if the
holder's mailbox resolves under `hcs14`; **resumes** under an outstanding
reference if the account exists and the mailbox does not; and returns the issued
receipt on a replayed reference. The counter's record and the agent's record are
reconciled **from consensus and never from each other** — the counter learns what
each carried body became by reading the mirror under the transaction id it
recorded, which is also how the receipt's coordinates are filled. A provisioning
purchase that stops between rows is resumable from either side by reading the
ledger.

**`register_agent` is unchanged**: the agent is its own payer, out of the funded
fee, which is what T-P13-4 checks and what keeps `blurred` off its `hol`
resolution.

## Alternatives considered

- **(b) `doorbell` and `manifestTopic` become optional inside `provisioning`**,
  present exactly where the Postmaster created them, with §5.4's sentence widened
  to say that the provisioned path splits into a leg the Postmaster sells and a
  leg the agent performs itself. **This is the more accurate description of what
  D-159 attempted** and it is recorded as such rather than dismissed. It lost on
  cost, not on truth: the freeze has happened, so it is a **minor version** —
  0.6, across fourteen schema files registered on consensus and every wire string
  that names them (§1.7). (a) is chosen for this window because it needs nothing
  from the specification.
- **Move the provisioner into the counter.** Rejected: the topics are the
  agent's, signed with a key the Postmaster does not hold, so the counter could
  not build those bodies even if it wanted to (P-13). It would also have thrown
  away the one thing this arrangement demonstrates — that the payer really is a
  seam.
- **Have the counter parse a whole serialized transaction with the SDK's typed
  getters and sign the body it derives from that parse.** Rejected as the weaker
  guarantee, though it is more comfortable to write: it inspects one
  representation and signs another, and the two agree only because the same
  object produced them. The counter decodes the bytes it signs, so the argument
  to the policy *is* the argument to the signature.
- **Drop the receipt's provisioning line and price the account some other way.**
  Refused rather than offered, as it was when the item was raised: §14.3 has one
  schedule for everyone and the 2 ℏ is published under `provisioning`.

## Consequences

**No specification sentence changes, no schema changes, no wire string moves, and
no test is added.** The version stays 0.5.9. This is a build decision of D-165's
kind: what changed is which party pays for bodies the specification already
describes.

- **CLAUDE.md §11**'s provisioning order becomes boot → `buy_stamp` with
  `provision` (the transfer, then the mailbox with the counter as payer, then the
  receipt) → `register_agent` → `resolve` self; and its payer-seam paragraph
  records that the remote half is exercised, for provisioning bodies under a
  purchase reference.
- **LIMITATIONS L-5** becomes "carry exists only inside the purchase — the
  transfer and the provisioning it sells". **T-P4-2 stays untested**, because
  `send`'s submissions still use the local payer. L-14 is unchanged.
- **`provisioningFieldsThisCounterCannotFill` stops refusing on its own**, and is
  kept as the assertion that the counter can fill every field the registered
  schema requires. It still reads the schema, so a field added there stops the
  sale before the quote rather than after the transfer.
- **Two clauses of D-159 are superseded and neither is edited away**: its step
  3's "all paid by the operator's key under D-157's seam", and its rejection of
  "have the Postmaster provision and register on the agent's behalf" — which was
  rejected on T-P13-4 *for the registration*, and that half of it stands
  unchanged and is why `register_agent` is still the agent's own act.
- **New modules**: `app/src/counter/body.ts` (a `TransactionBody` decoder),
  `app/src/counter/carry.ts` (the policy), `app/src/counter/context.ts`, and
  `app/sdk/carry.ts` (the remote `Signer`). `core/protokey.ts` moved out of
  `app/sdk/` because both parties now read it and a Postmaster module must not
  import a Correspondent one.
- **The fee ceiling is per row and read from `networks.ts`** — the same file the
  Correspondent builds each row's cap from — with an operator override,
  `WISHMAIL_CARRY_MAX_HBAR`, that may only lower it. This was not the first
  design: a flat 2 ℏ ceiling looked prudent and would have refused the doorbell,
  the first row of every provisioning purchase, **after the transfer had landed**.
  `check:correspondent` caught it offline. A fee-gated topic creation was
  observed to fail at a 20 ℏ cap and to succeed at 100 ℏ charged far less
  (`networks.ts`, FETCHED 2026-09-08), so the Postmaster's authorised exposure per
  doorbell is 100 ℏ and its actual charge is what the network charges. That is
  stated rather than buried.
