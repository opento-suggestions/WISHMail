# D-157 — The sender signs the ring; who pays is the sender's choice; and the payer is a seam

Status:      accepted
Date:        2026-09-09
Decided by:  Sonic
Drafted by:  Claude Code
Sections:    §4.4, §6.1, §6.4, §13.2 (F-7), §15.3 (L-5)
Tests:       T-P2-4 added. T-P4-2 unchanged.
Supersedes:  D-47 in part — the Postmaster is the default payer, not the required one
Spec marker: CHANGED: D-157 on §4.4, §6.1, §6.4

## Context

Two sentences of the frozen text disagreed about who submits a first-contact
connection request, and the disagreement was raised as ledger §G item 14 on
2026-09-09 rather than coded around.

§6.4 step 1 read, literally: "one stamp passes to the Postmaster, the Postmaster
submits the HCS-10 connection request paying that stamp as the doorbell's fee."
§4.4's own MUST fixes something narrower: a doorbell "MUST carry a HIP-991
custom fee of exactly one stamp, in the stamp token, collected by the treasury."

And the 2026-09-08 probe (D-149, ledger §H) established by observation, not by
reading, that **HIP-991 debits the transaction payer** — two otherwise identical
submissions to one fee-gated topic, one paid by a stranger and one paid by the
operator with the stranger signing, put `effective_payer_account_ids` at the
stranger and at the operator respectively. So whoever pays the ring must hold
the stamp at that moment, and the question "who submits" is the question "whose
stamp is spent".

The two readings cost the same one stamp to the same treasury. They differ in
hops, and in whether a stamp passes through a third party's hands.

## Decision

**The sender always signs the connection request. Who pays for it is the
sender's choice, as it is for every other submission the sender makes. The
Postmaster is the default payer and what the reference Correspondent uses.**

D-47 — "the Postmaster pays; the agent signs" — is **partially superseded**: the
Postmaster remains a payer the sender *may* use and is no longer one it *must*.
The half of D-47 that is not superseded is the half CLAUDE.md §3 names as a
non-negotiable: the Postmaster holds no agent key, ever.

§4.4's transfer-then-ring sentence generalizes from "the Postmaster" to "the
payer": a self-paying sender rings in one hop; a sender that borrows any payer
transfers one stamp to it first — bearer custody in transit, not key custody —
and rings in two. **The ring costs the sender one stamp either way**, which is
what P-7 and §17.3 are actually about: the mailbox is not a public forum, and
what the fee secures is that only stamped mail reaches the door.

F-7 now reads "for every submission", the connection request included. L-5 is
narrowed to a first contact whose payer the sender borrows.

**A new MUST, on the Postmaster, mirroring T-P13-3 on the purchase leg:**

> A Postmaster MUST NOT submit a connection request that the requesting agent
> did not sign.
> `Conformance:` T-P2-4 — a fixture connection request bearing only the
> Postmaster's signature is refused at carry and reaches no doorbell.

The mirror is exact and deliberate. On the purchase leg, T-P13-3 makes the
buyer's signature over the whole body the thing that makes the purchase the
buyer's; here the agent's signature over the request body is the thing that
makes the ring the agent's. In both cases the Postmaster's own signature is a
payment and never an authorship.

**The build consequence is the seam.** Every consensus submission an agent makes
is constructed *agent signs, payer signs*, with the payer an injected signer: a
local key now — the operator's, from that operator's own configuration (D-156) —
and a remote signature through the Postmaster's `carry` (§6.1) later. Nothing
above the seam knows which. It is built as a seam and not as a shortcut because
the same code path has to serve a self-paying sender, a borrowed operator, and a
Postmaster.

## Alternatives considered

- **(a) Rule for §6.4's literal reading: the Postmaster submits.** Lost because
  it makes the Postmaster's liveness a condition of first contact for every
  sender, including one whose account can pay its own fees — which F-7's own
  last sentence says is never the case.
- **(b) Rule for the sender submitting, and amend §6.4 to say so.** Lost for the
  narrower reason that it would forbid what §3.5 permits: a sender that wants
  the Postmaster to pay for a ring, as it may for any other submission.
- **Leave it ambiguous and let the implementation choose.** Rejected under
  CLAUDE.md §5: an ambiguity a build has to resolve is a specification defect,
  and coding around it is what §5 forbids.

## Consequences

§4.4, §6.1 and §6.4 carry `CHANGED: D-157`; F-7 and L-5 follow them without
markers, being §13 and §15 restatements of what those sections fix. §A gains
**T-P2-4** and the register moves from 83 to 85 with D-160's row (80 core + 5
extension); the extract-and-diff passes both ways.

`app/src/tools/send.ts` keeps `SenderContext.ringer` and its meaning changes:
it was a competing reading held open, and it is now the payer, injected. Where
supplied, that account pays and submits the request the sender signed; where
absent, the sender does both itself.

**A patch, not a minor version.** No wire string moves and no registered schema
changes on this decision's account (§1.7). T-P2-4 is a new test against a new
MUST, which invalidates no previously conforming implementation, because nothing
conforms: T-P9-2 blocks every claim while `spec/pins.json` carries 28 unfilled
pins.

What it leaves open: nothing in §19. The MVP defers T-P4-2 — a Correspondent
completing `send` through the reference Postmaster's carry — for reasons of our
own scoping, stated in LIMITATIONS.
