## Context

Q-12 asked whether the stamp should be fungible or a collectible, and D-20 had ruled the question be settled by measurement rather than argument.

## Decision

The record, from ledger §B row D-49, verbatim:

> Q-12 closed: the stamp is fungible (HIP-991 fees are HBAR or fungible HTS only; simplest).

## Alternatives considered

- A non-fungible stamp (HIP-17/412) — rejected on the measurement: a HIP-991 custom fee is payable in HBAR or a fungible HTS token only, so a non-fungible stamp could not pay a doorbell.

**Citation added 2026-09-07 (D-135).** Q-12 was closed on that measurement, and the measurement was an inference until §1.6 pinned HIP-991. It holds, and the stamp is fungible for the reason given: `hip-991.md:88` — "A custom fee can be set in HBAR or HTS fungible tokens and must have an accountID as the receiver" (`hashgraph/hedera-improvement-proposal` @ `03701720`, blob `179b61be…`). A non-fungible stamp could not denominate the doorbell's fee. FETCHED from here on.

## Consequences

Closes Q-12. §14.4 and L-12 state what follows: a spent unit returns to the treasury indistinguishable from unsold supply, so 'this stamp' means the postage a settlement affixed, under its postmark, and a spent unit has no history to resell.
