## Context

Three competing conventions publish agents in the DNS, and picking the wrong leaf would have made the profile unreadable to everyone.

## Decision

The record, from ledger §B row D-72, verbatim:

> The DNS address is `dns:<fqdn>` (RFC 4501), not `ans://`: DNS-AID-02 (May 2026) publishes agents at plain owner names and `_agents`/`_index._agents` SVCB leaves, AID uses `_agent` TXT, and the `_ag` label lives only in ANS's convergence claim — three drafts, three labels, so WISHMail owns one TXT leaf under whatever name the agent has and pins none of them. Also dissolves feasibility item 19.7 (no ANS registration needed; `agentfacts` takes a URL directly, so no NANDA index read is required).

## Alternatives considered

- `ans://` as the address scheme — rejected: the recon found no `ans://` anywhere in the live NANDA code, and the `_ag` label lives only in ANS's convergence claim.

## Consequences

The address is `dns:<fqdn>` (RFC 4501) and WISHMail reads exactly one leaf under it: `TXT` at `_wishmail.<fqdn>`, a tag=value list in the manner of DKIM and DMARC (§9.3). Reading one leaf of our own naming avoids adjudicating between DNS-AID, AID and ANS, which is P-6 applied to the DNS.
