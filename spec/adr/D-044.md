## Context

HIP-991 collects a custom fee in one token, and P-11 requires one price for every buyer. Read together they force the stamp to be a single token, and its issuer to be whoever holds that token's supply key.

## Decision

The record, from ledger §B row D-44, verbatim:

> The pedestal: the stamp is one token and the Postmaster is whoever holds its supply key. Forced by HIP-991 single-token fees + P-11. Delegated vending is a Q-7 question.

## Alternatives considered

- Several stamp tokens, one per deployment or per registry — rejected: a HIP-991 fee names one token, and P-11 would then have to hold across tokens that trade against each other.

## Consequences

The Postmaster is defined by what it holds rather than by who it is (§3.5): there is one stamp, and the Postmaster is whoever issues it. Who that party should be, and whether a second may vend from the same supply, is Q-7 and stays open (§19.3).
