## Context

§5.5 and §5.6 wrote the AAD's fields as `{p, v, ledgerTag, lane, resolutionProofHash, nonce}` while §7.2 — the normative section — fixes `{p, v, l, lane, rp, nc}`.

## Decision

The record, from ledger §B row D-127, verbatim:

> **The AAD's key names are §7.2's everywhere.** §5.5's Envelope and §5.6's rebuild rule wrote `{p, v, ledgerTag, lane, resolutionProofHash, nonce}` while §7.2 — normative — fixes `{p, v, l, lane, rp, nc}`; under RFC 8785 those are different bytes and a different `id`. §7.2 wins (§12.1). §5.6 now also says where a reader's `v` comes from: the wire string of the minor version the chunk's `s` (schemaRef) is registered under (§1.7). T-P1-4's vectors are the court; no test changes. The most consequential seam of the pass.

## Alternatives considered

- None recorded. The ledger §B row states the decision without a fork; where a fork was closed, the row says so.

## Consequences

Under RFC 8785 those are **different bytes**, so the two spellings would have produced two different envelope identifiers for the same envelope. §7.2's key names are now used everywhere, and `v` is sourced from the minor version the chunk's `schemaRef` is registered under (§5.5, §5.6, §1.7). `spec/vectors/aad.json` is the court (T-P1-4), and this is the reason it has to be.
