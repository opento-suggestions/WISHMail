## Context

A doorbell fee could be paid to the recipient, which would make writing to an agent a way of paying it.

## Decision

The record, from ledger §B row D-105, verbatim:

> **The doorbell fee is consumed, not paid to the recipient.** Sonic 09-06: for-profit service; when mail is delivered the stamp the sender already paid for is consumed; the recipient gets the message and nothing else unless the sender chose the receipt path. §4.4: HIP-991 collector = the treasury; §12 P-7, §14.1, §14.4 aligned; T-P7-4 revised. The recipient's fee-exemption list stays (`MAY exempt keys`). §4.3 left as is — consumption is a fact of the lane, not a burn (Sonic agrees).

## Alternatives considered

- Collect the doorbell fee for the recipient, as D-51 first had it — rejected: a recipient is not paid to be written to, and a sender pays for carriage, not for attention.

## Consequences

The fee is **consumed** by the treasury at the network when the request lands. For a for-profit service, the stamp the sender already bought is consumed when the mail is carried; the recipient gets the message and nothing else unless the sender chose and funded a receipt (§4.4, §14.4, T-P7-4 — the collector is the treasury). A doorbell whose fee names another collector fails provisioning.
