# D-159 — Provisioning has one order, and the two new verbs are affordances rather than tools

Status:      accepted
Date:        2026-09-09
Decided by:  Sonic
Drafted by:  Claude Code
Sections:    §4.6; §5.4 and §14.3 by the 2026-09-09 addendum below
Tests:       none added. T-P13-4 and T-P16-1 are what the order is built against.
Supersedes:  none
Spec marker: CHANGED: D-159 on §4.6 (×2), §5.4, §14.3

## Context

§4.6 names two provisioning paths and what each produces. It does not say in
what order the acts happen, and the order is not free.

An agent needs an account, a doorbell, a log, a manifest topic, a declaration,
and — to resolve under `hol` without `blurred` — a registration on an anchor
that admits it (§9.5, D-110). HCS-10's `register` operation names an account
(`index.md:419-420` at blob `0cb5d2eb`, FETCHED 2026-09-09), and §9.5's rule
reaches the agent's profile *through that account's memo* or through a `t_id`
the registration carries. A registration submitted before the topics exist
therefore lists a box that is not there.

Separately: the MVP needs two verbs the six do not cover. goose has to be able
to say "make me a mailbox" and "register me", and §6.1's six verbs act on one
noun — an envelope — while these two act on an agent's own provisioning.

## Decision

**The order, per agent.**

1. **Boot.** The Correspondent process generates the agent's keys on first run —
   never before, never elsewhere (P-13, §3.3) — and prints the public half.
   Nothing is written to consensus.
2. **`buy_stamp`**, with `holder` the agent's **public key**. The stamp transfer
   to the key alias auto-creates the account under that key (HIP-32/HIP-542,
   ledger §H), owned by the agent, holding stamps and no ℏ. This is T-P16-1's
   mechanism, and it is why **the account is bought, not funded.** *(Amended
   2026-09-09, below: this transaction carries a third leg, the registration fee,
   so the account is born holding stamps and exactly one fee.)*
3. **`generate_mailbox`.** The agent creates its doorbell with §4.4's one-stamp
   HIP-991 fee collected by the treasury (T-P7-4), its log, its manifest topic
   (T-P17-3), its HCS-2 declaration registry and its HCS-1 profile file — the
   file with no admin key, per D-150 — and sets its account memo (§9.2). All
   owned by the agent's keys (T-P17-1); all paid by the operator's key under
   D-157's seam. It is the Postmaster-agent's own 2026-09-08 provisioning
   replayed for a third party.
4. ~~**Fund one fee.** The operator transfers to the agent's account exactly
   enough ℏ for the registration transaction and no more. This is the **sole**
   exception to an agent holding no ℏ, and it exists because step 5 requires
   it.~~ **Folded into step 2 by the 2026-09-09 addendum below.** The exception
   itself stands: the agent holds ℏ exactly once, for exactly one submission.
5. **`register_agent`.** The agent submits HCS-10's `register` on the HOL
   testnet anchor **as its own payer and its own signer** — which is what
   T-P13-4 checks ("payer and `account_id` are both the agent's; no registration
   in the suite is paid by the Postmaster") and what makes §9.5 resolve it
   **without `blurred`**.
6. **`resolve`** of the agent's own address succeeds under `hcs14` and under
   `hol`.

**Both new verbs live on the Correspondent MCP**, because the agent has to be
able to say them. **Neither is added to §6.1's six and neither is on the
conformance surface.** §6.1 fixes six verbs on one noun and the class/invariant
matrix of §1.4 is unchanged; T-P15-4 requires those six to be identical across a
release's transports, not that a release offer only those six. They are
documented in §4.6 as the reference implementation's provisioning affordances.

§4.6 also gains the sentence that ties step 4 to its test: whoever pays for an
agent's provisioning MAY fund exactly that one registration fee and no more —
funding is a payment and not a party (§3.9, D-48).

## Alternatives considered

- **Register first, then provision.** Rejected on HCS-10's own reading: a reader
  who follows the anchor reaches the agent through the account, so a registration
  that precedes the topics resolves to nothing until they exist.
- **Add `generate_mailbox` and `register_agent` to §6.1.** Rejected: §6.1's
  surface is what every class is tested against and what T-P15-4 compares across
  transports. Two verbs that only a self-provisioning agent ever calls would
  widen the conformance surface for a convenience.
- **Have the Postmaster provision and register on the agent's behalf.**
  Rejected on T-P13-4 for the registration — a Postmaster-paid registration
  resolves with `blurred` and the test forbids one in the suite — and preferred
  against for the rest, because the self-provisioned path with a borrowed payer
  is the one the MVP can actually demonstrate end to end.
- **Fund the agent generously and let it pay for its own topics too.** Rejected:
  an agent holding ℏ is a standing exception, and the point of the seam (D-157)
  is that the agent never needs one except where a test requires it.

## Consequences

§4.6 carries `CHANGED: D-159` and gains the order paragraph, the two
affordances, and the fund-one-fee sentence with its `Conformance:` note naming
T-P13-4. No new test: no new MUST is added — the MAY about funding is a
permission, and the requirement it points at is T-P13-4's, which already exists.

No schema and no wire string moves. A patch under §1.7.

`app/sdk/` builds the two affordances beside the six tools, and its config
template carries the operator's payer for steps 2 through 4.

## Addendum — 2026-09-09, the registration fee is priced into the purchase

**Ruled by Sonic, 2026-09-09, the same day as the decision.** §4.6 already said
the Postmaster-provisioned path "MAY prepare the agent's registration for the
agent to submit under its own key, as payer, and MAY fund that fee". Ruled that
it does.

**Step 4 folds into step 2.** The provisioning purchase is **one atomic
transaction with three legs**:

1. ℏ from the buyer — the C-operator — to the Postmaster, for the price;
2. `$POSTAGE` from the treasury to the agent's public-key alias;
3. the registration fee, in ℏ, from the Postmaster to that same alias.

The account is born holding stamps and exactly one fee, in one act, and one act
leaves the demo. The agent's ℏ story is unchanged and is the point of the whole
arrangement: it holds ℏ once, to sign its own name on the anchor, and never again
(§3.5, D-156).

**Why it is one transaction and not two.** A Hedera `TransferTransaction` carries
several transfers atomically, so the alias is created once and credited with both
assets in the same act; two transactions would let the second fail after the
first succeeded, leaving an account with stamps it cannot register and a
provisioning run that has to be resumed rather than retried. It also makes the
fee visible as part of what was bought: one `txRef` in the receipt, and every leg
of the purchase under it.

**What it carries.**

- The `PriceList`'s `provisioning` entry gains `registrationFee?` (§14.3), so what
  the Postmaster funds is published before it is charged, like every other number
  it charges.
- The `StampReceipt`'s `provisioning` line gains `registrationFee?` (§5.4), so a
  Verifier reading the receipt sees the fee as a leg of the purchase and not as a
  gift arriving from an account the receipt does not name.
- Both are schema changes and both are admissible only while `registeredSchemas`
  is null (§1.7, D-161), which is why they land before the freeze.
- T-P11-4 remains the court for both: a receipt's `registrationFee` equals what
  the price message current at the purchase names.
- `generate_mailbox` and `register_agent` are unchanged in what they do. Only who
  funded the fee moved, and it moved from a transfer beside the purchase to a leg
  inside it.

A Postmaster that does not fund the fee prices none and records none; §4.6's
permission stays a MAY and this addendum says what the reference Postmaster does
with it.
