# D-136 — A rate-priced method carries `rate` and no `unitPrice`; `StampReceipt` gains `rate`; prices are decimal strings

Status:      accepted
Date:        2026-09-07
Decided by:  Sonic
Drafted by:  Claude Code
Sections:    §5.4, §14.3
Tests:       none added, none amended. T-P11-4 is unchanged and becomes true — see Consequences.
Supersedes:  none
Superseded:  none
Spec marker: CHANGED: D-136 on §5.4 and on §14.3

## Context

Standing up the reference Postmaster's first price list forced three questions that §14.3 leaves underdetermined, and they turned out to be one question asked in three places: how a method priced by reference to another asset is **priced**, **quoted**, and **receipted**.

1. **The receipt had nowhere to put the rate.** §14.3 says "the receipt records the rate used and when." §5.4 gave `StampReceipt` no `rate` field. `spec/schemas/stamp-receipt.schema.json` tracked §5.4 and carried a `$comment` raising the gap as a decision candidate.
2. **`unitPrice`'s units were unstated.** §14.3 said only "the price of one stamp in the method's asset." x402's own `PaymentRequirements` carries `amount` in atomic units, so atomic was a live reading.
3. **`unitPrice` on a rate-priced method meant nothing coherent.** If the rate derives the amount at purchase, a published `unitPrice` is a snapshot of a number nobody charges. Meanwhile T-P11-4's third branch — "the referenced rate applied to the **reference price**" — named a reference price that was not a field of anything.

A fourth thing surfaced beside them. `spec/schemas/price-list.schema.json`, written at the outfitting on 2026-09-07, read §14.3's `payTo | facilitator` as exclusive and encoded `oneOf`. That is wrong, and it would have rejected the very message this deployment publishes.

## Decision

**Prices are decimal strings in the asset's natural unit.** Not atomic units, which bake a network's decimals into a document a Verifier reads; not JSON numbers, because §5.1 canonicalizes with RFC 8785 and a float is a hazard. `"0.10"`, matching the record's own `$1.00` bundle. Conversion to atomic units happens where it belongs — when the x402 `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` is issued (§14.2).

**A rate-priced method carries `rate` in place of `unitPrice`, never both.** `rate` becomes `{source, pair, reference {amount, asset}}`: `reference` is the price of one stamp in the reference asset, and `source` and `pair` name what the Postmaster reads at purchase to convert it. `unitPrice` is the fixed-price field and is always in the method's own asset.

**A bundle's price follows its method's pricing basis** — the method's own asset where the method is fixed-priced, the reference asset where it is rate-priced — so the rate converts a bundle at purchase exactly as it converts `reference`. This is what lets one bundle, twelve stamps for $1.00, be offered on both legs (§4.5's uniformity).

**§5.4's `StampReceipt` gains `rate? {source, pair, value, at}`**, present exactly when the method that bought the stamps is priced by reference: `value` is the rate the Postmaster read and `at` is when it read it. This closes §14.3's sentence.

**§14.3's two pipes are read differently.** `payTo | facilitator` is **inclusive**: §14.2 has the `PAYMENT-REQUIRED` carry "the Postmaster's receiving address," which *is* `payTo`, so an x402 method needs it alongside `facilitator`, not instead of it. `unitPrice | rate` is **exclusive**: a method priced two ways publishes a number nobody charges. The schema encodes `anyOf` for the first and `oneOf` for the second.

Sonic, 2026-09-07, RECORD, on all of it.

**Version: 0.5.1.** A patch, and the window for it is narrow and open now. §1.7: a patch "changes no wire string, and **once a minor version's schemas are registered** it changes no schema." Nothing is registered under HCS-13 — `spec/pins.json`'s `registeredSchemas` are all null, which is what T-P9-9 tracks — so a schema-touching correction is patch-level today and would be 0.6 after registration. No wire string moves: `$id`s stay `urn:wishmail:0.5:…`, the AAD's `v`, the HPKE `info` and `wireStrings` all stay at `0.5`.

## Alternatives considered

- **Atomic units for `unitPrice`.** Rejected: it bakes per-asset, per-network decimals into a document humans and Verifiers read, and the price list is not the wire format of a payment.
- **JSON numbers rather than strings.** Rejected: the message is canonicalized under RFC 8785 and a float is a hazard.
- **A publication-time `unitPrice` snapshot on a rate-priced method, with `rate` beside it.** Claude Code's initial reading, MINE. Rejected by Sonic: it publishes a number nobody charges, which §14.3's own MUST forbids — "MUST charge exactly what the price message current at the purchase yields."
- **Leaving the receipt without `rate` and treating §14.3's sentence as loose.** Rejected: the sentence is normative and the field is cheap. The blast radius is confined to `buy_stamp`'s output, because §14.2 puts the purchase off the record — "WISHMail records postage spent, not postage bought" — so no Verifier path, no AAD, no evidence bundle, and no observed object is touched.
- **Reading `payTo | facilitator` exclusively**, as the schema did. Rejected on §14.2's own sentence.

## Consequences

**T-P11-4 needs no amendment, and that is the point.** Its sketch already reads "`count × unitPrice`, a bundle's price at its count, or the referenced rate applied to the reference price." The third branch named a field that did not exist; `rate.reference` gives it a home. Three branches now map to three fields exactly. The test becomes true rather than being changed, which is why this decision registers no test and moves none.

Two schemas follow the specification, as §1.7 requires and never the reverse. `stamp-receipt.schema.json` gains `rate` and its `$comment` changes from raising the gap to naming the test that holds what JSON Schema cannot: the receipt does not carry the method, so a schema cannot check that `rate` is present exactly when the method is rate-priced. T-P11-4 is the court. `price-list.schema.json` moves `unitPrice` out of `required`, relaxes `payTo`/`facilitator` to `anyOf`, adds the exclusive `unitPrice`/`rate` constraint, and gives `rate` its `reference`.

The reference deployment's first price list is what this was decided for: an `x402-usdc` method carrying `payTo` **and** `facilitator`, `unitPrice` `"0.10"` and a bundle of twelve at `"1.00"`; and an `hbar` method carrying `payTo`, no `unitPrice`, `rate` naming SaucerSwap and `HBAR/USD` with `reference` `{"amount": "0.10", "asset": "USD"}`, and the same bundle quoted in USD.

Nothing here is required of a Verifier or a recipient. §14.3's MUST — publish before charging — is untouched.
