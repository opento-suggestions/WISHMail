## Context

§14.3 fixes the price topic as "an HCS topic whose sole submit key is the Postmaster's and whose memo is `wishmail:prices:1`." D-101 (2026-09-06) settled *that* the submit key is the Postmaster's sole submit key. Neither says **which** Postmaster key, because at the date of D-101 the Postmaster was one identity with one key and the question did not arise. D-140 has since given the Postmaster three accounts and three keys, so the question exists and must be answered before the topic is created — a topic's keys are set at creation and a sole submit key cannot be repaired into a different one without an admin key that also has to be decided now.

**D-137 does not exist as a prior ruling on this**, and this is worth saying plainly because it was asked. The ADR series ran D-1 – D-134 before this phase; D-135 onward are numbers proposed in the Phase B plan. There is no earlier text on the price topic's key to conform to or to contradict. The nearest prior ruling is D-101, quoted above.

## Decision

**The price topic's sole submit key is the operator key, and its admin key is the operator key** (RECORD, Sonic 2026-09-07: "the price topic's submit key is the operator key").

The price list is **the service speaking** — not the treasury and not the Postmaster-agent — so its submit key is the service's signing identity. The other two candidates are wrong for reasons worth recording:

- **Not the treasury.** The treasury is a pool. Its key is hot because §14.2's atomic purchase needs it on every buy (D-140), which is a different job with a different exposure profile; a key that signs thousands of transfers should not also be the key whose signature makes a price authoritative. Nothing about holding stamps qualifies an account to say what stamps cost.
- **Not the Postmaster-agent.** That identity is a Correspondent with its own doorbell, log, and manifest topic (D-140). It is the service's *customer-facing peer*, and the price list is not something an agent says to another agent — it is the operator's published schedule, which §4.5 requires be "identical for every buyer." Putting it under the agent identity would blur an operational identity into a normative one.

The **admin key is the operator's too.** §14.3 needs no admin key to function, but a price topic with none can never have its memo corrected or its keys rotated, and unlike the doorbell there is no invariant that wants immutability here. The doorbell's fee schedule key is omitted (D-138) because §4.4's "exactly one stamp" should be structural; nothing analogous holds for a price topic, whose whole content is *meant* to change over time — every new price is a new message. P-17's own text says an admin key "may be rotated and is never cleared," which is the posture taken.

**No key of any agent appears on this topic**, and D-47 is not in tension: D-47 forbids the operator's key on an **agent-owned** topic. The price topic is not agent-owned. It is the Postmaster's own, and §14.3 says so — "the Postmaster's" — which is exactly why the operator key belongs here and on nothing else in D-138's table.

## Alternatives considered

- **The treasury key.** Rejected above: wrong job, wrong exposure.
- **The Postmaster-agent key.** Rejected above: it makes a normative publication look like an agent's utterance.
- **A dedicated fourth key for publishing prices.** MINE, rejected: it splits an authority that §14.3 treats as single, and it adds a key to protect with no invariant asking for it. If Q-7 later separates price authority from carriage authority, that is the change that should introduce the key — with a reason.
- **No admin key, matching the doorbell.** MINE, rejected: the parallel does not hold. The doorbell's immutability makes §4.4's fee structural; a price topic's content is designed to change, and stranding its memo would be a loss with no offsetting invariant.

## Consequences

The price topic is created after `$POSTAGE` — the first `PriceList` names the token (§14.3's `stampToken`), so the token must exist — with `submitKey` and `adminKey` both the operator's, memo `wishmail:prices:1`, and no custom fee. Verification reads it back from the mirror node and checks the memo, that `submit_key` and `admin_key` are the operator's, and that `custom_fees` is empty.

The topic ID goes to `app/deployment/hedera-testnet.json` and, when a claim is first made, into the claim as `prices {ledgerTag, topicId}` (§5.10). It does **not** go into `spec/pins.json` — §18.4 enumerates that file's contents and the price topic is not among them (D-144).

**D-101 is refined, not superseded.** D-101 ruled the topic's shape and the reading rule; this names the key it left unnamed. Both stand, and neither file gains a supersession pointer, because nothing D-101 says becomes false.

**Q-7 stays open, unchanged.** §19.3 asks "how the supply key and the price topic's submit key change hands" — handover, not birth. This ADR decides which key exists at creation and says nothing about transferring it, which is the question §19.3 holds for a later version. That the two questions are adjacent is exactly why the distinction is recorded rather than assumed.
