# D-162 — One template, three readers

Decided by:  Sonic
Drafted by:  Claude Code
Sections:    none — §11.7 already fixes the narrative's discipline; this extends it
Tests:       none added, moved or amended

## Context

Three surfaces say what happened to a letter, and until now each would have said
it in its own words.

1. The lines `send` logs as consensus facts land — the live account, watched
   while the tool blocks (D-30: `send` blocks).
2. The text block `send` returns beside its structured `Postmark` — the
   retrospective account, read after the fact.
3. `narrate()`'s sentences over an `EvidenceBundle` — the reading of the
   evidence, produced by any Verifier from public data (§11.7).

§11.7 already binds the third: "The reference implementation generates its
narrative from fixed templates over bundle fields, one sentence per fact, so
that this is demonstrated rather than promised." The first two had no such rule,
and they are the two a user actually watches.

The specific hazard is vocabulary. §2.3 reserves *delivery* for the lane — "No
sentence in this document says an envelope was delivered to a recipient" — and
§11.8 forbids turning silence into refusal, expiry into non-delivery, or an
unclaimed receipt into a returned letter. A progress line reading "delivered to
Bob" would be the one place in the system where the vocabulary is not the
specification's, and it would be the place a judge looks first.

## Decision

**The three come from one template file.** Same sentence, three readers. A fact
gets one form of words, and the live account, the retrospective account and the
narrative cannot drift from each other or from the bundle.

**None of the three may imply receipt or delivery.** Delivery is to the lane. A
receipt is the recipient's testimony and only exists when the recipient signed
one. Silence is silence.

Recorded as a build rule because it constrains three modules at once — the
Correspondent's logger, `send`'s return, and `verify`'s `narrate()` — and a rule
that lives in only one of them will be broken by the next person to touch
another.

**The rendering fact that makes the split matter, and that decides which surface
is which:** goose renders tool-call cards and final payloads and does **not**
render `notifications/progress`. So the Correspondent's own log is the live
surface, and the result's text block is the retrospective one. The MCP progress
notifications stay — they are correct, and another client may render them — but
nothing the demo depends on is only visible there.

## Alternatives considered

- **Three independent sets of strings.** Rejected: it is the drift that is the
  problem, and three sets of strings is a guarantee of drift.
- **Generate the log lines from the bundle too.** Rejected as impossible in the
  live case: the bundle exists only after reconciliation, and the log lines are
  written while the facts are landing. One template over two shapes of input is
  what the decision asks for; one input is not available.
- **Rely on `notifications/progress` for the live account.** Rejected on the
  FETCHED rendering fact above: a surface the demo's client does not render is
  not a surface.

## Consequences

No specification sentence changes. `app/` gains one template module; `send`,
the Correspondent's logger and `verify.ts`'s `narrate()` read from it. §11.7's
MUST — a narrative carries its bundle's digest (T-P3-4) — is unaffected and
still holds only of the narrative.

Written into `CLAUDE.md` §11 so that it binds the next module that wants to
print a sentence about a letter.
