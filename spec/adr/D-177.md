# D-177 — §11.5's table wins: a resolution that does not replay leaves the envelope UNVERIFIED, not unbound

Status:      accepted
Date:        2026-09-11
Decided by:  Sonic
Drafted by:  Claude Code
Sections:    none. No normative text changes; ledger §A's sketch for T-P6-1 is amended.
Tests:       T-P6-1 — sketch amended. No test is added or removed; the register stays at 87.
Supersedes:  none
Spec marker: none — see "Why this carries no CHANGED marker" below.

## Context

Two authorities in this repository disagreed about one rung of §11.5's ladder.

Ledger §A, row 51 (`spec/CONFORMANCE_TESTS_v0_5.md:51`), the sketch that is the
scope of T-P6-1:

> A resolution proof whose inputs are altered after resolution no longer hashes
> to the proof; the envelope appraises **unbound**.

§11.5's table (`spec/WISHMAIL_SPEC_v0_5.md:1700`), which is what a Verifier
implements:

```
resolution replays under a claimed profile                 unverified   T-P6-1, T-P12-4
```

A conformance body cannot follow both. The sketch fixes what the test is *for*;
the table fixes what a Verifier *does*. Where they differ, one of them is wrong,
and until it is settled the row cannot be expanded at all — which is how this was
found: the derivation table for the conformance bodies
(`conformance/DERIVATION.md`, finding F-3) could not place T-P6-1 and recorded it
as a ledger question rather than guessing.

## Decision

**The table is right and the sketch is amended to match.** T-P6-1 appraises the
envelope `unverified`.

## Why

**The specification says it twice more, in prose, in the same section.** §11.5's
reading paragraph (`:1681`):

> a bound and stamped envelope whose resolution could not be replayed is still
> certified mail whose address is unappraised; an unstamped envelope is not
> certified mail; an unbound envelope does not open.

That is a ladder of three different failures, and it places a failed replay on
the top rung deliberately.

**§A's own footer already reasons this way, about the neighbouring row.** On why
T-P6-7 is keyed to P-6 rather than P-1:

> every P-1 row of §11.5's table yields *unbound* while a manifest not found at
> its location yields *unverified* — a proof nobody can find leaves the envelope
> bound and its address unappraised.

T-P6-1 is the same shape: a proof whose inputs no longer hash is a proof nobody
can replay. The letter is still welded to its header, its lane and its postage —
P-1's welds are untouched — and what is unknown is *who the address belonged to*.
Calling that `unbound` would say the letter is not certified mail at all, which
is a strictly stronger claim than the evidence supports, and P-12 forbids
appraising above what the evidence shows in one direction and requires the
minimum in the other.

**And the invariant keying says it.** T-P6-1 serves P-6 (Resolution is a proof),
not P-1 (Binding). §11.5's unbound rungs are P-1's and P-10's and P-17's; the
unverified rungs are P-6's, P-9's and P-12's. A P-6 row yielding unbound would be
the only one of its kind.

## Consequence

**No code changes.** `app/src/tools/verify.ts` put `T-P6-1` under `unverified` in
`REASON_STANDING` and was right; the sketch was wrong. This is the first ruling in
this ledger where the reference implementation is the thing that was correct and
the register is the thing amended, and it is worth saying plainly: the sketch was
written before the table it now agrees with.

T-P6-1's conformance body follows the amended sketch.

## Why this carries no CHANGED marker

The `CHANGED: D-nnn` convention marks an amended paragraph of
`spec/WISHMAIL_SPEC_v0_5.md`, and **no normative text changes here**. §11.5's
table already said `unverified`; §6.2's `Conformance:` note (`:928`) names T-P6-1
without naming a standing; §12.2's P-6 entry names it in a range. The only line
that moves is ledger §A's, and the ledger is not normative.

**It still rides a version, and the reason is §5.10.** A conformance claim names
`suite {version, date, reportDigest}`, and §A's sketch is the scope of the test
the suite runs. A suite at 0.5.12 and a suite at 0.5.13 test T-P6-1 for different
standings, and a reader comparing two claims must be able to tell which. So the
patch number moves even though the normative document does not — which is the
narrow case §1.7 does not speak to, and is recorded here rather than assumed.

## Alternatives

**Amend the table to say `unbound`.** Rejected: it would contradict §11.5's own
reading paragraph, break the ladder's three-way distinction, and make one P-6 row
behave like a P-1 row. It would also be a normative change where a non-normative
one suffices.

**Leave both and let the body choose.** Rejected: a sketch that disagrees with the
text it courts is exactly the defect §1.3 exists to prevent — no requirement
without a test, and no test that tests something else.
